The SDK 54 `expo-splash-screen` config plugin owns the native launch layer and points both light and dark appearances at the cache-distinct 1320x2868 `mapbound-native-splash.png`; the deprecated top-level Expo splash path is intentionally absent. `LaunchLoadingOverlay` renders the visually matching, lighter `loading-screen3.jpg`, and both layers preserve the artwork with `contain`, preventing the baked logo from being stretched on different iPhone canvases. The native layer remains image-only because platform splash screens cannot run reliable React animation. `App` mounts `LaunchLoadingOverlay` above its database/font gate on the first React render; `MapScreen` later reports its composite readiness upward, while the root-owned dismissal flag keeps the player and reward queue gated through the fade. Startup work may therefore continue behind the opaque presentation but cannot postpone its subtitle or prompt. `LaunchLoadingOverlay` is an explicit presentation state machine. Once its local image is decoded, it subtracts elapsed JavaScript-runtime launch time from the 1000ms artwork-only budget; a slow native/bundle handoff therefore begins the subtitle immediately instead of restarting the full second. Three real `Animated.Text` nodes then share a 1050ms native-driver opacity/vertical-offset stagger: cyan Walk, gold Explore, and parchment Reveal. A saved-language update changes those live phrases without restarting animation. After completion, a 500ms quiet beat precedes the 360ms fade-in of Press to start and its restrained native-driver pulse. Readiness and preparation feedback are deliberately hidden before player intent. If the prompt is pressed while ready, one guarded 800ms root-opacity animation reveals the already mounted map and invokes `onStart` only after the fade. If pressed while unready, `startRequested` alone exposes the localized loading row; the splash remains opaque until readiness triggers that same idempotent fade automatically. The font and phrase gap scale from device width while remaining capped at the established size. The version is independent of the footer, rendered at six points in the safe bottom-right corner after the clean-background budget. Reduce Motion retains the budget, quiet beat, and final fade but publishes subtitle and prompt motion at their accessible final states and suppresses the pulse. Keeping the subtitle in React Native rather than the bitmap allows English/French switching without duplicating the large portrait asset.

Atlas icon-bar destinations share the preloaded `atlas-page.wav` player at 50% volume. Opening a destination triggers the CC0 book-page recording from `AtlasScreen`; `MapScreen` invokes the same player immediately before every destination-to-map close path, so header back, swipe-back, accessibility escape, Android back, and map-focus actions remain synchronized without adding a second player. The volume adjustment is isolated to that page player, leaving ink, reward, and medal feedback unchanged. The global Expo Audio session enables iOS Silent-mode playback and `mixWithOthers`, keeping feedback audible through the phone speaker without pausing or ducking external audio.

`explorerScore.ts` derives score from authoritative cell sets and the permanent expedition-seal count instead of persisting a mutable counter. It removes known `loop_fill` cells from the walked set, re-derives every currently fillable contour under the gameplay area cap, unions persisted and derived enclosure cells, then awards one point for each unique discovered cell, one enclosure bonus for each enclosed cell, and 200 points for every expedition seal. This makes historical coverage and expedition completions score immediately, lets active cells update the total live, and stays duplicate-safe across reprocessing, backup restore, relaunch, and later reconciliation. `areaComparisons.ts` bundles 55 sorted international reference surfaces and their source URLs so `ExplorerScorePanel` can show a current equivalence and next milestone without network access. `MapScreen` passes the same derived total to Details and the idle Field Log, adds source counts to the Details breakdown, and labels enclosure and expedition stamps with their exact awards. The idle `WalkControls` row pairs that score with today's pedometer total; an active recording replaces the idle pair with distance, duration, and current-walk steps. Start and Stop share one centered 60%-width, 44-point-high control, followed immediately by the GPS status strip in both states.


`AtlasCabinet.tsx` owns the shared textured full-screen shell, left back control, gold icon/title/subtitle header, Cinzel display typography, section rules, Reduce Motion preference, transient objective stamp, and locally bundled page, ink, and reward sounds. Its three app-lifetime audio players begin loading when the module initializes, avoiding per-award module import and player-construction latency. A reward starts the ink impact on the same artwork-ready boundary as the stamp and schedules the jingle 90ms later; replay seeks the retained player to its start. `App.tsx` configures Expo Audio with `mixWithOthers`, so game feedback neither pauses nor ducks external music and podcasts, and loads the OFL-licensed variable font before exposing the application; dense data and body copy stay on the system face. Ordinary Atlas and map cards use neutral borders, while gold remains semantic for selection, progress, rewards, emblems, and primary actions. `AtlasHudDecor.tsx` supplies non-interactive paper-grain and line/diamond primitives to the main-map medal, objective, transient scope, navigation, and walking surfaces. The four persistent surfaces compensate for most of the overlay padding to form separate stripes with 7-point screen gutters and restrained 10-point outer corners. The city medal stripe is 46 points high; the navigation stripe is 56 points high and frames 48-point-high, 40-point-wide regular controls with one-point gaps and padding, while two-point hit slop preserves effective 44-point widths. The objective flag remains an independent accessible action embedded at the medal stripe's right edge and shares its single paper-grain layer. Those overlays use Cinzel only for identity labels and names; percentages, step counts, GPS accuracy, and other changing values remain in the system face. Engraved translucent-gold states replace flat fills, and GPS status color is confined to its dot and label. The stamp composes the transparent assets/ui/atlas-cartographer-stamp.png hand-inked seal beneath runtime title/detail labels, so one text-free asset serves localized city/district selection and completion messages. Its 106-point base is 20% smaller than the first generated-seal presentation; fitted 7.5/7-point title/detail text and their halo/drop-shadow copies remain inside one animated surface through the angled strike, compression, and spring settle. The surface stays at zero opacity until the current message's keyed local Image fires onLoad, then animation, sound, artwork, and lettering start from the same readiness boundary. Ordinary selection and zone-completion messages retain the ink sound. Reward messages select the offline `atlas-reward-jingle.wav`: `MapScreen` emits them once when the active closure monitor observes newly enclosed cells and once when a newly earned expedition seal appears. Both opt into the same 3x, map-centered, non-blocking presentation as a location selection, with the same strike and medium haptic. On iOS its `AtlasScreen` also owns the interactive left-edge back gesture: touches in the first 20 points reserve the gesture immediately, horizontally dominant drags beginning within 36 points capture after 3 points of movement, and the full surface follows the finger. Release completes after the smaller of 32% of the screen or 140 points, or after a fast rightward flick beyond 24 points. Cancellation springs back; Reduce Motion removes only the post-release completion travel. Atlas modals use transparent `overFullScreen` presentation to reveal the live map beneath them. History routes a detail swipe to its list first and disables swiping during a data operation. Map selection marks messages explicitly and scales the same stamp to 3x while MapScreen measures its top and bottom panels to center the animation in unobstructed map space; ordinary completion messages keep the standard stamp. Details, History, Completion, Expeditions, Medals, Options, recording detail, and diagnostics opt into the shared shell. Stop confirmation and the recording summary use a compact textured Atlas dialog variant with ornamental rules; its hold-to-quit control owns one 1300ms deadline shared by fill and confirmation, rechecks elapsed time on release if the timer was delayed, tolerates small finger drift, and pairs a high-contrast bordeaux-to-dark-orange fill with a light leading edge and completion haptics. Recovery retains its specialized presentation. `ExplorationMap` owns the separate map-native effects: a 520ms new-cell ink color reveal and a 900ms vertex-bounded focused-route draw. A native map touch contracts the enlarged wordmark; returning from any Atlas page resets it to the enlarged state until the next map touch. A separate 280ms recording-layout progress drives the wordmark height and opacity to zero, which naturally lifts the medal rail, objective flag, and objective ledger, while the Field Log removes its six-point dock gap. Starting a walk also locks the underlying wordmark into its compact state so stopping reveals the compact title rather than replaying the return presentation. Reduce Motion publishes all final layout states immediately.

`AtlasNavigationProvider` in `MapScreen` supplies the active page, language, and one navigation dispatcher to every modal portal. `AtlasNavigationDockLayer` owns the absolute bottom coordinate, horizontal margin, and safe-area padding for both the map and every `AtlasScreen`; each surface mounts the same layer contract, preventing coordinate or geometry changes at the modal boundary. The layer is deliberately outside `AtlasScreen`'s entrance animation and the former map-dock return spring is removed, so content transitions behind a visually stationary dynamic menu. `AtlasNavigationDock` renders the same seven destinations inside that layer. Map is an explicit destination with a dedicated icon, so the map state expands its localized label exactly like every selected Atlas page. On the map, `WalkControls` reserves the shared 56-point footer height and renders above it. Regular-width controls are 48 points high and 40 points wide with two-point hit slop; below 360 points they compact to 34 points wide with five-point hit slop, retaining effective 44-point targets while fitting all seven destinations and the selected label. Atlas screens reserve footer plus safe-area height in their content container so actions and final rows remain reachable. A page marks its destination selected, switches directly when a different icon is tapped, and can return through the explicit Map destination or the established selected-page/back behavior. The map variant recognizes a 320ms long press on inactive destinations, retains the localized preview label for 650ms after release, fades it over 220ms, and suppresses that gesture's normal press navigation. Page footers do not use hold previews. History passes its protected-operation flag through `swipeBackDisabled`, disabling both gesture and footer transitions until the operation completes.
`App.tsx` restores the persisted `appearance_mode` before mounting the map and updates a live appearance palette before triggering each mode render. `createAppearanceStyles` builds paired registered style sheets so existing static styles across every component resolve to either Explorator or Daylight without duplicating layouts. The status-bar foreground follows the selected appearance, and removed legacy Custom values safely fall back to Explorator through the validated appearance-mode reader.

Sound and haptic preferences use `sound_enabled` and `haptics_enabled` rows in the existing `app_settings` table. Missing values default to enabled for upgrade compatibility. `App.tsx` restores both before mounting the map, while `feedbackPreferences.ts` provides immediate in-process gates for the preloaded Atlas players, lazy medal player, map-selection haptic, stamp impact, and medal-success notification. The Options switches persist independently; disabling either channel never blocks its associated navigation, animation, or reward lifecycle.
On iOS, `react-native-maps` uses Apple MapKit in dark `mutedStandard` mode for Explorator and light `standard` mode for Daylight. Appearance changes remount the native map at the retained camera region. Native POIs and the native user-location cursor remain disabled so game-owned annotations define the playfield. Medal landmarks use an independent 0.14 maximum latitude delta, twice the former far-level cutoff, while route endpoints and heavier overlays retain their stricter render thresholds.

Migration 32 adds Forbidden Zones as independent user-created world state. Migration 33 repairs development databases whose numeric migration ledger collided with another branch, and startup also enforces the same idempotent schema invariant against the real SQLite catalogue rather than trusting ledger presence alone. Migration 34 adds nullable `forbidden_zones.comment`, with an idempotent catalogue check for development ledgers. Migration 35 removes orphaned `forbidden_zone_cells` left by an affected earlier removal path and invalidates derived completion snapshots when it repairs data; startup repeats this idempotent repair in case a development migration ledger reused the numeric id. `forbidden_zones` stores the immutable merged geometry snapshot, cell-derived area, optional normalized single-line comment of at most 120 characters, and timestamps; `forbidden_zone_cells` stores the exact 15 m grid membership with one owner per cell and a spatial `(cell_x, cell_y)` index. Comment edits update only zone metadata and never invalidate completion, while geometry/cell snapshots remain immutable. Neither table has a session or administrative-zone foreign key. Recording deletion, route reprocessing, loop-fill rebuilding, and OSM boundary refresh therefore cannot remove, relabel, or expand a Forbidden Zone. Full local-data deletion and Backup V5 replacement intentionally replace this world state.

The repository awaits database initialization before native Forbidden Zone reads and writes. Creation and removal use atomic exclusive transactions with bounded retry only for transient SQLite busy/locked contention; permanent schema or uniqueness failures roll back and surface a specific user-facing reason. Removal deletes child cell rows explicitly before the zone row rather than depending only on SQLite cascade state, and creation defensively clears any already-orphaned cells before inserting. Backup replacement and full-data deletion use the same child-first ordering. Exact cells are inserted in conservative bounded batches so large valid zones remain within native binding limits.

`findEnclosedExplorationRegionContainingCell` uses the same grid-contour topology that supplies exact loop candidates, but rasterizes only the closed hole containing the held cell and stops after the configured safety count. `classifyEnclosedAreaForLoop` is the shared policy boundary: at most 150,000 m² remains a normal loop candidate, while only a larger exact enclosure may become forbidden. The Field Log tool is disabled during recording. While selected, `MapScreen` routes a map hold exclusively to removal lookup or Forbidden Zone analysis, so administrative long-press selection cannot also run. Creation is capped by the named 2,000,000 m² safety constant and persists exact cells plus merged polygons; `ExplorationMap` renders those polygons as a dedicated translucent-purple layer rather than as explored cells.

When Forbidden Zone mode is inactive, administrative selection tests the exact held coordinate first. If no eligible boundary contains it, `buildMapZoneSelectionProbeCoordinates` supplies four concentric 16-direction map-space probe rings scaled to the current viewport and capped at 120 metres. Sampling the interior radii prevents a low-zoom outer probe from jumping over a nearby valid district. District fallback probes are restricted to exact official districts belonging to the detected city, which improves low-zoom finger accuracy without crossing the Lyon/Villeurbanne boundary or changing high-zoom exact selection. Forbidden Zone polygons remain explicitly non-tappable and ignore pointer events, so their dedicated purple rendering cannot consume normal administrative holds. A separate map-tap coordinate lookup uses the exact persisted cell set to reveal one React Native map-marker label over the tapped purple position. The label contains the optional comment and area, opens the editor when tapped, and is keyed to the active district objective so unrelated map taps do not dismiss it but a district/city change does.

Forbidden Zone hydration is deliberately outside the launch-critical saved-map query batch. The launch gate becomes ready from the existing core map data, then loads the independent purple layer after dismissal; likewise, an uncached administrative completion scan cannot begin while the branded launch overlay is active. This keeps an upgrade or invalidated completion cache from stalling the Start interaction.

Completion first retrieves Forbidden Zone cells inside the evaluated boundary’s grid bounds, then tests each cell center against the current administrative polygon and holes. The resulting overlap is subtracted from the cached raw zone total; explored cells remain the numerator and forbidden cells are never added to it. The same stored zone can therefore cross city or district boundaries without owning either one. Creation, deletion, and restore clear `zone_completion_snapshots` and advance the walking exploration revision so visible objective results recalculate immediately. Previously earned `zone_achievements` remain permanent, but current percentage and remaining-cell displays use the restored denominator after removal.

Explorer Score receives the hydrated union of exact Forbidden Zone cell snapshots as an explicit exclusion set. `calculateExplorerScore` removes those keys independently from walked GPS/inferred inputs, persisted loop-fill cells, live derived enclosure cells, the discovered-area union, and its surface-area calculation. This is deliberately defensive in addition to the storage separation: a later walk, repaired legacy data, or restored overlap still cannot turn excluded territory into points while its Forbidden Zone exists. Removing the zone makes any genuine exploration evidence eligible again without modifying that evidence.

`gps_observations` retains every validly formed raw fix, including filtered fixes, so a later out-of-order delivery can deterministically derive the route again in timestamp order. `route_snapshots` freezes validated render geometry for finalized sessions. `pending_recording_repairs` is a durable finalization outbox: its row is created in the same transaction that ends a valid session and removed only after route and exploration caches are safely present. `pending_recording_discards` temporarily hides a stopped session with fewer than two accepted points for a five-minute late-GPS recovery window, then removes it if it is still underfilled.

`zones` caches country, city, and district boundary polygons fetched from OSM administrative relations. Local queries request body-plus-geometry output for the containing relations, nearby relations, and every district inside the containing city; country results remain bounded display data. V2 relation assembly joins unordered and reversed member ways, retains multiple outer components and holes, assigns an exact district only when nine-by-nine-grid interior samples from every outer component fall inside the same city, and marks any incomplete or degenerate assembly as a display-only bounds fallback. Rendering and objective recovery repeat that geometric parent check instead of trusting persisted parent IDs, preventing a shared boundary vertex or detached component from importing a neighboring level-9 relation. Repository upserts never replace an exact OSM polygon with a fallback response. `MapScreen` first restores the saved objective as a failure-safe, then captures only the first point delivered by the foreground-location lifecycle (never the separately restored durable player marker). Before enabling launch dismissal, a one-shot resolver uses exact cached boundaries or one OSM fetch, selects the containing official level-9 district before its city, saves it through the ordered objective chain, and installs the matching `MapBoundaryContext`; denied or unavailable location, failed fetches, and unresolved city containment preserve the restored objective. Automatic launch selection suppresses the manual ink stamp. `MapScreen` stores city and district overlays in one `MapBoundaryContext`, serializes context swaps, and uses a latest-generation guard. Once an objective exists, a render gate exposes administrative overlays only when that context belongs to the objective city. When the city id changes, it first publishes `EMPTY_MAP_BOUNDARY_CONTEXT`, waits two animation frames, then mounts the latest requested city and districts. `ExplorationMap` also keys the native MapKit view by city identity and remounts it at the last visible camera region, guaranteeing removal of any retained previous-city polygon without clearing explored data. It renders every district exactly once with a 1.5-point muted copper stroke, strengthens only the selected district to 3 points, and renders the current city once with a 3-point muted wine perimeter that strengthens to 4 points for a city objective. Both objective scopes share a quiet parchment selection wash, leaving burnt orange for explored city territory, pale yellow for countryside territory, and gold for today's city discoveries and active routes. Viewport updates never mutate the objective or boundary group. A map long-press resolves exact cached city/district containment, falls back to one explicit OSM fetch when needed, rejects stale concurrent responses by request generation, gives haptic feedback, and saves objective changes through an ordered persistence chain. If no city/district contains the exact hold but an exact country does, `shouldSelectCountryside` clears the objective and publishes the localized countryside rail and stamp; no containing country is treated as open ocean and ignored. `shouldOfferMapZoneScopeChoice` compares the held city with a current city derived from the selected city objective or active `MapBoundaryContext`. A same-city district hold applies that district immediately and clears the transient picker. When the held city differs and includes a district, the picker exposes both scopes; an existing city objective keeps City as the initial cross-city scope, otherwise District remains the initial scope. When a refresh repopulates a previously missing objective zone, the map reloads the saved objective from the restored cache.
The `zones.admin_level` column preserves the source hierarchy. Level 9 is the minimum and final playable district tier for France and other places using the same hierarchy. Level 10 remains cached with parent geometry only for source fidelity and backup compatibility; repository district lists, map containment, objective saving, completion eligibility, refresh result counts, and visible achievement rollups admit only level 9. Migration 26 adds the column without deleting zones or achievements and invalidates refresh state; legacy rows remain hidden until a one-time location- or objective-centered fetch classifies them. A known level-10 saved objective is deleted from settings, clears the HUD/selection/boundary group, and cannot be saved again, while its achievement row remains stored for archival and Backup V5 compatibility.


Migration 18 consolidates legacy non-walking session rows, exploration cells, loop fills, active markers, and imported backups into the walking profile without deleting recordings. Legacy activity preferences and completion objectives are removed because the app no longer exposes activity choices.

`zone_cell_totals` caches calculated zone denominators by zone id, cell size, and deterministic geometry fingerprint. A changed boundary invalidates its previous total instead of reusing a stale denominator. Objective numerator reads use the explored-cell coordinate index and the selected polygon's grid bounds before contour and point-in-polygon work, so a district switch scales with local coverage rather than the complete lifetime ledger. District expedition opportunity reads apply the same boundary-first rule to cached street segments.

`zone_achievements` stores the first exact 100% completion evidence independently from the mutable zone cache. Achievements therefore remain earned after a boundary refresh or cache clear. `zone_refresh_state` stores the last attempt, last success, status, and error needed for the 30-day automatic refresh policy and visible diagnostics.

`district_expeditions` stores the deterministic daily choice identity, district/date/slot, goal kind and target, durable progress, and acceptance/abandonment/completion timestamps. `expeditionDefinitions.ts` owns a 25-entry catalogue and uses a district/date-seeded Fisher-Yates shuffle to select five non-duplicate viable entries per local day. Street and medal opportunity counts remove impossible catalogue entries before selection. Migration 30 removes the former global partial-unique index, so multiple accepted unfinished expeditions persist concurrently while their timestamps, progress, abandonment, and completion remain independent. Migration 31 rebuilds the original four-kind-constrained table using the shared complete kind list, preserves all durable rows and child evidence, verifies expedition foreign keys, and clears only untouched offers for the current local date. The next district load therefore forces an immediate five-choice expanded rotation without deleting accepted, abandoned, completed, or sealed history. `district_expedition_seals` permanently records completed missions and is counted directly for the retroactive 200-point-per-seal Explorer Score contribution.

`expeditionProgress.ts` derives spatial missions from finalized post-acceptance 15 m cell evidence: cardinal adjacency supports frontier, gap-sealing, and density rules; a district bounding box supports compass, three-by-three sector, central, and outer-belt rules; and point-to-boundary distance supports the 45 m border survey. Street progress uses durable 90% completion timestamps, medals use bounded acquisition timestamps, and `district_expedition_loop_evidence` links every applicable loop or staged mission to detected enclosures. Loop progress queries join only finalized sessions, and discarding an underfilled recording removes provisional evidence through its foreign key. Staged missions expose one progress step for each independently satisfied requirement. Reopening or repair therefore cannot double-count, and acceptance remains blocked during recording to make each evidence boundary unambiguous.

Backup V5 includes all 25 expedition kind identifiers, daily choices, active/completed state, finalized loop evidence, permanent seals, and exact Forbidden Zone geometry/cell snapshots plus optional comments. Both optional systems remain backward compatible: an older V5 manifest without either section restores it as empty state, while a v0.29 Forbidden Zone entry without `comment` restores that field as null.

`MapScreen` exposes Expeditions as a permanent compass tab in the primary navigation dock. It uses the same 44-point inactive target and engraved expanded selected state as the other destinations. `DistrictExpeditionModal` remains mounted only while visible, owns the shared Atlas screen/back gesture, lists every active mission (including other districts), and renders either the selected district dashboard or a no-district empty state whose action closes the journal before opening Completion. The objective HUD invokes the same open handler as a contextual shortcut and summarizes concurrent missions for the selected district.

`exploration_revisions` is advanced by SQLite triggers whenever explored cells are inserted, updated, or deleted. `zone_completion_snapshots` stores each full city/district result against that revision and the deterministic boundary geometry fingerprint. The map mirrors snapshots in memory, restores a matching value without a polygon rescan, and warms the paired district/city result in the background. Live unfinished-recording previews remain memory-only and cannot create durable achievements or snapshots.


Startup treats the active-session marker and unfinished `walk_sessions` row as authoritative. It drains the durable background outbox, verifies and stops any inherited native task to form one coherent checkpoint, reloads the complete persisted trace, recalculates distance from confirmed GPS segments, restores the original start time/mode/session into the normal active HUD, and then starts one managed foreground watcher plus permitted background protection on that same session. Walking steps are queried again from the original start timestamp when the device pedometer permits historical reads; denied or unavailable motion data stays at the existing reliable value and is never invented. Cold task callbacks resolve the active session from `app_settings` instead of depending on module memory. If foreground permission, metadata, or service teardown prevents a safe automatic resume, Recovery V2 presents the bounded saved-route preview and Active/Interrupted/Uncertain fallback actions. Resume, Finish, and Discard remain available there; failed actions keep the authoritative marker and restore protection where possible.

## Recording Flow

1. Startup initializes saved data and authoritative active-recording restoration while requesting foreground permission behind the branded launch overlay. The Start control remains unavailable until SQLite restoration has either resumed the unfinished walk or produced the explicit Recovery V2 fallback. After map, data, recording state, permission, and bounded initial-location readiness complete, the overlay waits for the user's explicit entry tap.
2. With permission granted, one managed foreground-location hook requests the current position and keeps an idle high-accuracy watcher active so the player marker is available before recording.
3. Native watcher errors retry with bounded backoff. During a recording, a watchdog probes and replaces a watcher that stops delivering usable fixes.
4. User taps Start only after recording-state initialization proves that no authoritative unfinished session exists.
5. The new `walk_sessions` row and its active-session recovery settings are created in one exclusive SQLite transaction.
6. The managed watcher switches to best-for-navigation settings. Step counting and background tracking initialize independently.
7. Foreground fixes enter the canonical SQLite queue without an intentional reorder delay, with a file-journal fallback on storage pressure or failure. Every delivered native background batch is sorted, written to a temporary document-directory file, and atomically renamed into the durable outbox before database initialization or queueing. A cold callback first resolves the persisted active session; ownerless fallback points are matched only when exactly one recording contains their timestamp, and unmatched recent batches remain journaled for the bounded recovery window. Active backlogs are admitted in 512-point chunks so the in-memory queue cannot starve their tail.
8. Each queued fix first enters `gps_observations`. In-order fixes use an incremental fast path; a late or improved fix rebuilds contiguous `gps_points` from all raw observations and requests one full UI synchronization. The UI otherwise reconciles the database tail every three seconds. This makes arrival order irrelevant while retaining the complete trace as stable route chunks of at most 256 raw vertices; only the newest 300 points remain in diagnostic/movement state.
9. Exploration cells and trusted distance advance only across confirmed live segments. Suspicious outage gaps split the live route, contribute neither explored cells nor distance, and remain subject to the existing finalized validation system; the renderer never invents a straight diagonal through missing movement.
10. User taps Stop, then chooses Continue or holds Quit in the confirmation dialog.
11. Confirmed Stop hides the active UI, invalidates recording ownership, verifies the serialized native background task has stopped, waits for entered handlers, drains the durable outbox, flushes the chronologically ordered queue, and atomically finalizes the SQLite session. The recovery marker is compare-cleared only after that boundary succeeds; a core failure restores the active recording. As soon as the durable boundary succeeds, confirmed live cells, the History row, headline stats, the recording summary, and Start control return without waiting for derived work. An underfilled session is hidden as a recoverable discard tombstone instead of being deleted before a delayed native callback can supply its final point.
12. Route snapshot inference, exact pedometer reconciliation, medal safety evaluation, objective recalculation, and the full saved-data refresh run asynchronously after the durable Stop boundary. A route made entirely from confirmed adjacent fixes takes a direct snapshot fast path and never queries the surrounding 450m street corridor; only suspicious gaps invoke cached-street graph inference. The pending repair row keeps route/exploration recovery crash-safe. A native event that enters JavaScript after finalization still journals before session lookup and uses its recording-owner hint, the persisted active marker while it exists, or a unique timestamp match as a last resort. ## Exploration Cells

The current exploration layer uses configurable 15m x 15m grid cells.

GPS paths are first classified into path segments:

- confirmed GPS segments
- rejected gaps

Confirmed GPS geometry marks direct cells. A suspicious gap marks cells only when the frozen snapshot contains a validated street-graph bridge; rejected gaps are not sampled, so a missing GPS interval cannot create fake diagonal exploration through buildings.

Validated high- and medium-confidence street-matched bridge sections are stored as `inferred` cells. They contribute to the explored map, zone completion, and loop boundaries. Unmatched or low-confidence gaps remain rejected and never receive a straight-line fallback.

Normal startup reads distinct saved cell ids from the exploration ledger without loading saved GPS routes. The map mounts first; cached contour polygons are enabled only after the native map reports readiness. During recording, saved and active ids are combined before contour extraction so one surface owns shared boundaries and cannot show transient seams; unchanged sets retain their references so memoization avoids redundant work.

The 15m x 15m grid is still a temporary approximation before true OpenStreetMap street completion.
For rendering, adjacent explored cells are unioned into contour polygons instead of being emitted as overlapping rectangles. Each connected explored island becomes one native map polygon. Enclosed contours at or below the walking fill-area cap are rendered solid, which guarantees that small missing-cell channels cannot show through a qualifying discovered frontier. When a hole is filled, nested island contours inside it are discarded because the parent surface already covers them. Oversized enclosed contours remain explicit holes and receive their own black frontier, while filled holes leave no internal outline. Geometry-derived React keys force MapKit to replace a polygon whenever its holes change, including the transition from an open surface to a newly filled enclosure. The combined saved/live surface uses a matching edge stroke to cover native polygon rasterization seams.

## Loop Fill

Closed-loop fill is a gameplay-first V1 mechanic based on global walking explored-cell enclosure.

The app first samples trusted GPS path geometry into explored cells. Rejected GPS gaps never mark cells, so they cannot become part of a loop boundary.

All directly explored walking cells are treated as the boundary, even when they came from different recordings. The renderer and persistence layer share one authoritative grid-contour extraction: each qualifying enclosed contour produces the exact same cell set for surface rendering, loop-fill storage, and completion. The render-only city partition then colors that surface orange inside exact cached city bounds and pale yellow outside them; it never changes persistence or completion evidence. Nested contour cells are claimed once. The one-cell flood tolerance supplements only contours that are not already represented by the authoritative extraction.

Current thresholds are:

- minimum recording distance before loop analysis: 80m
- minimum enclosed cells: 1
- detection boundary expansion: 1 cell
- maximum enclosed area: 150,000m2

OSM is used as hidden analysis data inside the polygon. The app still measures walkable street length for future debugging and tuning, but OSM street density no longer blocks a valid loop from filling.

Walking exploration can contain multiple loop fills. Accepted loop-fill cells are stored separately from directly walked GPS cells.

## Street Completion

Street Completion V2 keeps Apple MapKit as the visible map while using cached OpenStreetMap ways as a durable analysis denominator. Progress is rebuilt only from immutable `route_snapshots`; raw GPS remains available solely to capture the one-time V1 migration baseline.

Flow:

- Fetch walkable OSM `highway` ways through the shared Overpass corridor service and split them into stable, at-most-35m local pieces.
- Build one spatial index over cached street geometry and sample each frozen confirmed or accepted inferred route at 3m intervals.
- Consider only OSM lines within 12m whose direction is compatible within 50 degrees, treating travel in either direction as valid.
- Select the nearest compatible line for each sample so parallel streets cannot both receive credit.
- Deduplicate coverage into 4m bins per stable OSM piece, persist each recording's covered-bin evidence, and union bins across recordings instead of adding repeat walks twice.
- Roll local pieces up to their parent OSM way for reached/completed street counts; the way is first completed when its loaded distance reaches 90%.
- Preserve the first 90% timestamp as audit evidence while deriving the visible completed count from current saved-route coverage, so deleting a recording also removes its contribution.

The one-time upgrade rebuild repairs historical OSM corridors asynchronously and then processes every valid frozen snapshot. Normal Stop runs the same aggregation inside deferred reconciliation; recovered finalization launches it without waiting; explicit Reprocess runs it after replacing frozen routes; deletion and restore schedule a fresh aggregate. - require every participating boundary fragment to form a non-degenerate closed ring for completion eligibility

## Street-Aware Inference

Street-aware path inference is persisted in an immutable route snapshot and is shared by route rendering, explored-cell generation, completion, and loop analysis.

The service projects suspicious GPS-gap endpoints onto nearby cached OSM street segments, jointly evaluates a bounded set of near-ambiguous endpoint snaps, and searches for the shortest plausible walking route. Ways tagged `foot=use_sidepath` are excluded so a parallel carriageway or tram/service line cannot displace its designated pedestrian sidepath. GPS-to-graph snap connectors remain part of detour/confidence evidence but are not counted as walked street distance for the routed-speed ceiling. New suspicious gaps first make one bounded 120m corridor request only where shared topology coverage is absent or older than 30 days. Each topology query starts at the primary FOSSGIS Overpass instance and fails over once to the independent Private.coffee global instance for HTTP 408/425/429/5xx responses, timeouts, network failures, or malformed JSON; non-retryable request errors stop immediately. Normal inference failures fall back to the cache behind a five-minute retry cooldown. This avoids both a history-sized fetch during normal finalization and treating a player beside the middle of a 35m fragment as if they were at one of its endpoints.

V3 graph edges preserve exact OSM nodes, add verified ground-level geometric crossings, and join grade-compatible fragment endpoints only within 8m. Bridge, tunnel, layer, access, and foot tags prevent common overpass and private-way false joins. Endpoint-join routes are capped at medium confidence. Only plausible high- or medium-confidence routes are frozen and counted, and a snap may close its visible endpoint seam only within 12m; longer, unmatched, or low-confidence gaps never receive a straight connector.

Each inferred snapshot segment persists schema-versioned evidence: acceptance reason, endpoint and intersection join counts, maximum endpoint seam, snap distances, gap duration/distance, routed distance, source topology size, confidence, and inferred-cell contribution. History exposes the accepted/high/medium/cell totals by default and the per-bridge evidence under Technical details. Once stored, a snapshot is never changed by map movement, cache refresh, normal saves, or loop recalculation; only an explicitly confirmed full-history or single-recording reprocess can replace it.

Explicit reprocessing is the only workflow allowed to replace existing historical snapshots. **Reprocess recordings** makes one consolidated Overpass linestring request covering every saved walking corridor; **Reprocess this walk** requests only the selected recording corridor, replaces only that frozen route, and then rebuilds the complete shared exploration/loop/street aggregate from all other unchanged frozen routes. This repairs the incomplete cache that caused the v0.3.50 legacy-freeze regression without returning to slow per-recording downloads. Each endpoint attempt has a 35-second client timeout, fetched street segments are batch-written atomically only after a complete successful response, and failure across both instances aborts the rebuild while leaving existing routes and progress untouched. One graph is then built per recording and reused for every suspicious GPS interval. During a full-history rebuild, individual recording calculation failures retain their previous frozen route while the remaining recordings continue. During a selected-walk rebuild, a calculation failure aborts with a visible preserved-route error so the action cannot report a false success. The complete candidate includes confirmed cells, validated inferred cells, and authoritative contour fills. If its unique-cell total is lower than existing progress, both snapshots and the explored ledger remain untouched and the result reports a safety stop. Otherwise all explored cells and loop metadata are replaced in one atomic transaction. The phased progress modal is displayed over the map after full-screen panels close, and any uncaught failure produces a visible error.

Legacy confirmed-only snapshots remain unchanged until the user explicitly reprocesses them. Backup V5 is a versioned binary record stream: magic header, compressed manifest, hot/archive session records, and a footer binding the manifest and ordered block checksums. The newest 20 sessions each receive a hot record. Older sessions are grouped by UTC month and split at 20 sessions or approximately 25,000 raw points; only physical storage is consolidated, while the manifest and each block retain every original session ID, metadata row, point stream, and route snapshot. Confirmed snapshot segments use compact raw-stream-position runs, deliberately avoiding any assumption that legacy `point_index` values are unique; inferred segments retain their full geometry, confidence, and bridge evidence. Each record has compressed/raw lengths and CRC32, and the footer verifies record order, totals, backup identity, and all checksums. Export verifies its cache copy, opens the native share sheet, then requires the user to select the externally saved Files copy; only a complete re-read with the same backup ID reports success. Restore first verifies the user-selected file and exposes manifest metadata/totals for a non-destructive preview. Confirmation re-reads that same cached file against its backup ID, checks authoritative active-recording state, closes file-journal admission, stops native tracking, settles the in-memory queue, then reads one block at a time and batch-inserts points inside one replacement transaction.
